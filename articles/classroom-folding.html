A paper-airplane STEM activity for teachers, parents and group facilitators

Group folding works best when the adult facilitator controls complexity, standardizes the starting material and gives the experiment one measurable question.

FoldFlight Lab Editorial DeskReviewed 16 August 2026How we research
For adult facilitatorsUse one model, consistent sheets, explicit orientation language and a simple experiment.

Choose a model you can teach aloud

Fold it yourself while narrating every orientation change before the session.

Standardize the paper

Give participants the same dimensions whenever possible so paper size does not become a hidden variable.

Use orientation words that survive rotation

Say “long edge toward you,” “colored side down,” or “open edge at the top.”

Add one STEM question

NASA’s paper-airplane activities use design testing and changes in weight distribution. Keep your version simple: change one variable, then measure one outcome.
