Five folding variables that change paper-airplane flight

A paper airplane is a coupled system. Small fold changes can alter symmetry, center of mass, wing shape and drag simultaneously.

1. Symmetry

If left and right wings differ in span, crease position or angle, the aircraft receives unequal forces and tends to roll or turn. Inspect it from the nose and from behind rather than only from above.

2. Center of mass

Nose folds place more layers forward. Too much forward concentration can produce a dive; too little can encourage pitch-up and stall. Change one nose feature at a time and repeat several flights.

3. Wing area

Broad wings can support slower gliding flight while narrow darts are usually designed to carry more speed. Hang time and straight distance are therefore different optimization problems.

4. Dihedral and trim

Slightly raised tips can improve roll stability. Tiny trailing-edge bends act like crude control surfaces. Millimetre-scale changes can matter because paper aircraft are light.

5. Launch

The throw is part of the experiment. Mark a start line, keep release height and direction similar, and do not credit a lucky throw to a design change.
