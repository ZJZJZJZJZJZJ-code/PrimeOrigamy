How to choose origami paper for planes and models

Paper choice is mechanical, not merely decorative. Thickness, mass, stiffness, finish and sheet geometry change the way a model folds and behaves.

Start with the job

A display model needs clean creases and enough stiffness to hold shape. A modular structure needs paper that tolerates repeated folds. A paper airplane adds mass distribution and bending stiffness to the problem. This is why an attractive pattern is a secondary criterion during learning.

Weight is not the entire story

Paper mass is often expressed in grams per square metre, yet two papers with the same stated mass can have different thickness and stiffness. Very soft paper can produce floppy wings; very stiff paper can punish small alignment errors. Test one sheet by making a sharp crease, reopening it and checking whether the surface cracks or springs back strongly.

Control dimensions

Traditional origami often begins from a square while paper airplanes commonly use A4 or letter rectangles. For experiments, identical starting dimensions remove a hidden variable. If you cut your own squares, measure carefully.

Buy small first

Start with a modest assortment. Record which size and paper type works for models you enjoy. Standardize only after repeated use shows what you actually prefer. A smaller set of well-understood papers produces better learning than a large collection of unknown materials.
