How to choose origami paper: GSM, size, stiffness and fold behavior

The right paper is not the prettiest sheet or the highest GSM. It is the sheet whose dimensions, stiffness, thickness and surface match the model you are trying to fold.

FoldFlight Lab Editorial DeskReviewed 16 August 2026How we research
Fast answerFor a first general-purpose pack, choose accurately cut square sheets around 15 cm with moderate stiffness and a surface that creases cleanly. Do not treat GSM as a quality score: thickness, stiffness, coating and model complexity matter together.

Start with the model, not the pattern

Paper is part of the mechanism of an origami model. A simple crane, a modular unit, a tessellation and a layered animal ask different things from the sheet. That is why “best origami paper” is usually the wrong question. A better question is: what properties does this fold sequence need?

Use casePrioritizeWatch out for
Learning basic foldsAccurate squares, visible front/back contrast, forgiving creasesVery tiny sheets or brittle coatings
Modular origamiConsistent dimensions and repeatable stiffnessMixed sizes that make units fit differently

Paper-airplane testingIdentical dimensions, mass consistency and flat sheetsChanging paper and geometry at the same time

GSM is useful—but incomplete

GSM means grams per square metre. It tells you areal mass, not the full mechanical character of a sheet. Two papers with the same GSM can differ in caliper, fiber structure, sizing, coating and stiffness. OrigamiUSA’s printer-paper review illustrates the point well: common office paper can serve as a useful baseline precisely because it is familiar and repeatable, not because a single weight number makes it “origami paper.”

A five-second sheet test

  1. Fold one edge carefully to another and check whether the sheet fights alignment.
  2. Sharpen the crease with a finger, reopen it, and look for cracking or surface damage.
  3. Hold the sheet horizontally by one edge and compare how much it sags.
  4. Fold two layers together and notice how quickly bulk builds.
  5. Write down the paper name or pack so you can reproduce a good result later.

Kami, printer paper and specialty papers

“Kami” is commonly used in the origami community for ordinary pre-cut origami paper. It is convenient for learning because the geometry is already square and many packs offer contrasting sides. Ordinary printer paper is inexpensive and globally available, which makes it a strong baseline for practice and experiments. Specialty papers become useful when you can name the constraint you are trying to solve.

Buy small before you standardize

A large pack is only a bargain if you enjoy folding it. Start with a modest quantity, repeat a few models, then standardize the paper that behaves predictably for your work.
