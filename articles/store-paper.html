How to store origami paper and finished models

Good storage protects appearance, but it also preserves the material condition you need for repeatable folding and paper-airplane tests.

FoldFlight Lab Editorial DeskReviewed 16 August 2026How we research
Storage goalKeep unused sheets flat, dry and clean; keep experimental aircraft identifiable.

Keep unused sheets flat

Use a rigid folder, document box or shallow drawer that supports the whole sheet.

Control moisture

Humidity can soften paper and change how strongly creases hold. Avoid damp rooms when repeatability matters.

Protect delicate finishes

Foil-backed, coated and textured papers can scratch, transfer marks or crack on tight folds.

Label experimental aircraft

Add a discreet code for paper type, version and date so nearly identical models remain comparable.

Replace fatigued test sheets

Repeated unfolding weakens crease lines and changes local stiffness. Use fresh sheets for controlled performance comparisons.
