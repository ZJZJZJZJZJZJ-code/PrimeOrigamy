How to store origami paper and finished models

Moisture, curl, dust and sunlight can change both appearance and folding behavior.

Keep unused sheets flat

Use a rigid folder, document box or shallow drawer large enough to support the entire sheet. Crushed corners and curled edges create unwanted geometry before folding starts.

Control moisture

Humidity softens many papers and can change how strongly a crease holds. Avoid damp rooms and direct sources of moisture, particularly when you want comparable paper-airplane tests.

Protect specialty surfaces

Foil-backed, coated and textured papers may scratch or transfer marks. Interleave delicate sheets when necessary and test a crease to see whether coatings crack.

Label test aircraft

Finished display origami needs dust and sunlight protection. Experimental aircraft need identification. Mark each configuration discreetly so nearly identical models do not become impossible to compare.

Replace fatigued sheets

Repeated unfolding weakens crease lines and changes local stiffness. Reuse paper for practice, but begin controlled performance tests with fresh sheets.
