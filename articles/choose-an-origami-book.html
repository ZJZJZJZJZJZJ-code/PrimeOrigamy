How to choose an origami book by skill level

A good origami book is a teaching system, not merely a gallery of finished models.

Inspect notation

Beginners should find clear explanations of mountain folds, valley folds, reverse folds and the symbols used throughout the book. Consistent notation reduces unnecessary cognitive load.

Look for intermediate states

Weak diagrams jump from before to after. Better books show how layers move between states and enlarge difficult details when necessary.

Check progression

A deliberate beginner book introduces a technique, repeats it, then combines it with something new. Scan several projects in sequence to see whether difficulty rises gradually.

Match material assumptions

Some complex models depend on thin specialty paper. Choose projects compatible with materials you can actually obtain.

Ignore raw project count

Twenty well-taught models can produce more transferable skill than one hundred poorly explained ones. Buy instructional quality rather than page-count theatre.
