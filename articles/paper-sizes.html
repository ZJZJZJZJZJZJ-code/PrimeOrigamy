Paper sizes for gliders, darts and origami

Sheet size is part of the design because it sets reference geometry, material quantity and structural span.

A4 as a baseline

A4 is standardized and readily available in Europe, making it useful for repeated paper-airplane experiments. Designs made for US letter paper may still work, but the aspect ratio differs and reference points can shift.

Why squares dominate origami

Squares create equal side lengths, useful diagonals and rotational symmetry. Many traditional bases rely on these properties. Pre-cut sheets remove a measurement step and improve repeatability.

Small sheets magnify errors

A one-millimetre alignment mistake represents a much larger fraction of a miniature model. Learn a new sequence at a comfortable scale before shrinking it.

Large sheets can become flexible

When sheet dimensions grow, ordinary paper thickness does not grow proportionally. Long wings and panels can therefore sag or twist. Changing scale means re-testing stiffness and trim.

For learning and measurement, a small set of standard sizes used repeatedly is more informative than constant novelty.
