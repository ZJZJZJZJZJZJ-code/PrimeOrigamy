Origami paper sizes: why 15 cm squares and A4 behave differently

Sheet size is not only convenience. It changes reference geometry, the scale of alignment errors, the number of millimetres available for detail and how flexible the finished structure feels.

FoldFlight Lab Editorial DeskReviewed 16 August 2026Research method

15 cm square: the common learning scale

OrigamiUSA identifies 15 cm as a common kami size. For many simple and moderate models, it gives enough room to see references while remaining easy to handle. It is also convenient because a pre-cut square removes one preparation variable.

10 cm square: errors become relatively larger

If a fold is off by 1 mm on a 10 cm sheet, that error is proportionally larger than the same 1 mm error on a 20 cm sheet. Small paper is excellent for compact modular units only when your alignment and sheet dimensions are consistent.

20 cm and larger: more room, different structure

Increasing sheet size creates more working room for layered sequences. But paper thickness does not scale with length. Large panels can feel relatively more flexible, so a model enlarged from 15 cm may need different paper stiffness.

Relevant route20 cm square paper · Amazon.de Affiliate-Link.

A4: useful for controlled paper-airplane work

A4 has fixed dimensions and is readily available in Europe, which makes it a strong experimental baseline. Designs originally developed around US Letter paper may still work, but the aspect ratios differ, so fold landmarks can shift.

Scaling rule of thumb

If you scale…Expect…Re-check…
SmallerHigher relative alignment error, tighter accessCrease accuracy, paper thickness at layers
LargerMore working room, more panel flexStiffness, wing / panel sag, trim
