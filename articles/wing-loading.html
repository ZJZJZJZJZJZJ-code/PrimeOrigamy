Paper airplane wing loading without the jargon

Wing loading connects aircraft weight to wing area. It will not predict every paper-airplane flight, but it gives you a useful way to compare designs and materials.

FoldFlight Lab Editorial DeskReviewed 16 August 2026How we research
Simple definitionWing loading compares aircraft weight with wing area. For paper airplanes, use it as a comparison lens—not as a promise that one number predicts the whole flight.

The ratio

In full-scale aerodynamics, wing loading is weight divided by wing area. A paper aircraft is far simpler and more flexible, but the ratio still helps explain why a compact, relatively heavy dart behaves differently from a broad, light glider.

Glider versus dart

A broad glider spreads its mass over more wing area and is commonly flown at lower speed. A compact dart concentrates mass into a smaller planform and is normally launched faster.

A practical comparison

  1. Fold the same design from two sheets with identical dimensions.
  2. Record paper type and, if available, sheet mass or GSM.
  3. Keep the launch point and direction similar.
  4. Run repeated distance and time-aloft trials.
  5. Inspect whether stiffness also changed wing flex or trim.

Why scaling breaks simple intuition

If you enlarge a paper model, its span grows but ordinary paper thickness does not scale proportionally. Larger wings may flex or twist more.
