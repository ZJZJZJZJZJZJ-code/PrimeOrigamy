Wing loading without the jargon

Wing loading connects aircraft mass to wing area and helps explain why similar paper planes can fly differently.

The ratio

In simple terms, wing loading is weight divided by wing area. Paper aircraft are far simpler than full-size aircraft, but the idea still provides useful intuition.

Glider versus dart

A broad light glider distributes mass across relatively large wing area and can operate at lower speed. A compact dart concentrates mass into a smaller planform and is normally launched faster. Neither is automatically superior because their missions differ.

A controlled experiment

Fold the same model from two papers with identical dimensions but different mass or stiffness. Keep folds, launch point and direction as similar as possible. Run several flights and record distance plus time aloft.

Scale matters

Making a model larger does not scale paper thickness with it. A large wing may flex more even when the geometric proportions are unchanged. Re-check wing angle and trim after changing scale.

Use wing loading as a comparison tool: ask whether the observed change came from geometry, material mass, or both.
